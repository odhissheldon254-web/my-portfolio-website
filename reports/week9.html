Week 09 — RFID & SPI

RFID Integration with Raspberry Pi Pico W

Week 9 introduced RFID, which is the first time in the course that a peripheral is not simply wired to the board. The tag has no battery and no connection at all — it is powered by the reader's own field and answers back through it. Getting a card's identifier onto a serial monitor is the whole exercise, and it builds directly on the SPI work from week 4.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand how passive RFID tags draw power from the reader's field.
  • Wire an MFRC522 reader module to the Raspberry Pi Pico W over SPI.
  • Read the UID from a card or fob and print it over serial.
  • Understand why the frequency band determines range and card compatibility.
  • Recognise what a UID does and does not prove about the person holding the card.

How a Passive Tag Works

The reader emits a continuous radio field. When a tag enters it, that field induces enough current in the tag's antenna coil to power its small chip. The chip then replies by changing how much energy it absorbs, which the reader detects as a signal. This is why a card works with no battery, and why it stops working the instant it leaves the field.

Frequency decides the behaviour. The MFRC522 used here works at 13.56 MHz and reads MIFARE cards over a few centimetres. The other common band, 125 kHz, uses entirely different tags — a card for one reader is invisible to the other, which is the first thing to check when a tag simply never appears.

Wiring the MFRC522 over SPI

SPI needs four signal lines plus a reset, and the module runs on 3.3 V. That last detail matters: the Pico is a 3.3 V part and the module is too, so they connect directly — but the same module wired to a 5 V board needs level shifting, and feeding it 5 V is a common way to destroy one.

MFRC522 Raspberry Pi Pico W --------- ------------------- SDA (CS) --> GP17 SCK --> GP18 MOSI --> GP19 MISO --> GP16 RST --> GP20 3.3V --> 3V3 (OUT) <-- never 5V GND --> GND

Reading a Card UID

With the reader wired, the loop is straightforward: ask whether a new card is present, and if so read its identifier. The UID is a handful of bytes, printed here as hexadecimal so it can be compared against a list later.

import board import busio import digitalio import time from mfrc522 import MFRC522 spi = busio.SPI(clock=board.GP18, MOSI=board.GP19, MISO=board.GP16) cs = digitalio.DigitalInOut(board.GP17) rst = digitalio.DigitalInOut(board.GP20) reader = MFRC522(spi, cs, rst) print("Hold a card near the reader...") while True: status, tag_type = reader.request(reader.REQIDL) if status == reader.OK: status, raw_uid = reader.anticoll() if status == reader.OK: uid = "".join("{:02X}".format(b) for b in raw_uid) print("Card detected - UID:", uid) time.sleep(1) # avoid reading the same card repeatedly time.sleep(0.1)

The one-second pause after a successful read is not cosmetic. Without it a card held near the reader is detected dozens of times a second, and every one of those would count as a separate event in an application that acts on a scan.

What a UID Proves

A UID identifies a card. It does not authenticate anyone, and it is not a secret — it is transmitted in the clear to any reader that asks, and blank cards that can be written with a chosen UID are inexpensive and easy to buy.

That is worth stating plainly before building anything on top of it. MIFARE cards do support real authentication, where the reader proves it knows a key for a specific sector before any data is exchanged. Reading a UID is the beginning of RFID work, not a finished security mechanism.

Reflection

The satisfying part of this week is that it makes SPI concrete. In week 4 SPI was a list of signal names; here those signals carry a real conversation with a chip, and the chip talks to something that has no wires at all.

The uncomfortable part is realising how weak identification-by-UID actually is. A system that unlocks a door because it recognised a number, where that number is broadcast to anyone who asks for it, is not far from a lock that opens for anyone who knows the shape of the key.
